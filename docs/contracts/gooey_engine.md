# The popup engine (gooey) -- contract

(The progress notes this contract names (`notifications_progress/`) are no longer in `docs/`: they are in git history,
`git log --all -- 'docs/agent_added_files_and_folders/notifications_progress/'`.)
(The gates, probes and fixtures this contract names without a directory went with the cleanup: tag `pre-cleanup` has them. What a change is proved with now is in `tools/dev/`, [tools.md](../tools.md).)

The engine's API for developers: every bar and popup as one surface, the slots (the popup, its child chain, the lane, the OSD,
the reel, the flourish), the content contract a popup's content speaks, and the rules a module meets when it hosts a popup.
What the user sees of it is on the shell's page, [../shell.md](../shell.md); the code is `modules/bar/gooey/`.

## Current state (2026-09-16) — the SDF engine, approved

The engine was rewritten (user's decision, 2026-09-14): every bar and every
popup is a BODY, a box, and all of them are drawn as ONE signed-distance
surface. Concave fillets, necks while opening and closing, void crossings,
corner migrations and the secondary popup all come from the same rules
instead of one hand-written Bézier case each. The user approved it on
2026-09-16; the pre-rewrite engine that had been kept for an A/B in
`test_bar.qml` (`modules/bar/gooey/legacy/`, the `ENGINE` switch) was deleted
with the approval. Where this doc says "the old engine", it means that one.

How the surface is made (round 2, "v2"; the full reasoning is in the file
headers). Round 1 blended the two nearest bodies with a smooth union; the user's
round-2 review showed what a pointwise blend cannot do (every join a circle,
whatever room it has), so the joins are now explicit geometry:

- **Host and guest.** A bar hosts a popup and a parent popup its child; two bars,
  or a bar and a child (a wide child clamped against a side bar), relate only
  where they touch, with nothing snapping, flaring or stretching between them;
  two popups never relate -- except as PEERS (The lane, below). Roles come from
  kind, not coordinate order, so all four edges draw the same joins. A bar's
  pieces settle and come in with how much popup there is yet; a popup's own
  pieces follow its box at once, so nothing trails behind it.
- **Snapping.** A popup face within `attachDistance` of a bar face it overlaps is
  drawn against it, letting go by `snapDistance` (the old engine's "wall snap",
  which also closes the hole a popup a few px under a perpendicular bar left). A
  popup END within `flushSnap` of its bar's end is drawn flush, letting go by
  twice that, so a pixel or two off is one straight edge. A bar's end that
  another bar covers (Bars in a corner, below) leaves no air, so nothing is drawn
  back from it and no end corner is made with it (the music popup in the corner of
  two bars was drawn 1.3 px off the top bar while it was short). Children never
  snap.
- **End corner.** A popup end pulled onto its bar's end makes ONE corner with it:
  one ellipse tangent to the popup's far face and the bar's side. Fully present
  it is the popup's own corner over its whole side, never turning tighter at its
  tip than half the blend radius (a surface-tension corner cut off at the popup's
  side was a point; half the BAR's radius, as it was until 2026-09-24, let a
  round-capped 6 px bar's popup turn at 1.7) and, where the side is too short for that, round -- never
  narrower than round, or its tip on the far face turns tighter still (3.9 at
  7 px); a few pixels tall, the bar's corner carries the lower part
  of the curve and the popup's end is drawn where it crosses the line. It
  replaced an end drawn at the rounded corner's tangent, which left a shelf (a
  notch) and, at no height, a hairline over the corner's air (round 4, the
  content-sized popup). A body with no area is not drawn at all -- the shader's
  coverage draws a zero-height box as a half-covered row -- but still rests on
  its bar, so its joins settle instead of letting go in one frame.
- **S-curve joins.** At each end the outline crosses the shared line from the
  inner body (whose end is within the other's span) to the outer one: the outer
  body's convex corner and a concave fillet on the inner body's side. The fillet
  is ONE ellipse solved for both of its S-curves at once (across the line and
  down the side) and for the corner it will really meet -- another join may have
  squared that corner, and fitting the fillet to the free corner and squaring it
  afterwards left the two visibly detached. Every claim on a corner is collected
  and resolved once, and one piece wins it whole (combining claims number by
  number threw a tight arc away): the piece of the TIGHTEST S -- a loose S's
  straight run takes any corner, a tight S's fillet only its own piece -- and
  among equally tight ones the piece that cuts the least area away (how far a
  flat ellipse's arc runs along a face is ill-conditioned). The music popup's far
  corner is claimed by the top bar's S down its side and the side bar's S along
  its foot; ranked by reach it went to the loose one, a notch of up to 3.9 px. With room
  they are quarter ellipses at their ceilings (the corner's own radius;
  `blendRadius` plus stretch) with a straight run between; with less room the S
  tightens instead of pinching -- both keep their height, meet at a shared
  tangent and straighten continuously to one edge at 0. Corners are ellipses
  with their own centre inset, so each half of a short S can leave its side
  tangentially and meet the other at an angle, and each cuts only inside its
  REGION (the rectangle its inset spans), so a corner may round over a whole
  side when its neighbour there is square.
- **Short popups.** The fillet is also a piece of a second S down the inner
  body's side, whose partner is that body's far corner; a popup shorter than
  both ceilings tightens that S the same way, so a 5 px popup is one long S from
  the bar to its foot instead of a flat fillet meeting a flat corner in a hairpin.
  While it is tight the far corner may take the whole side, as much as keeps its
  tip from turning tighter than half `blendRadius`: the S met a long, shallow
  surface-tension corner near its point, which read as an elbow at the top of a
  narrow S.
- **Resistance.** While a popup opens or closes, a join's tip on the bar stays
  toward where the popup will be open (`stickiness`, up to `stretchMax`, and
  `sideStretch` of that down the side), scaled by how far out the popup is ALONG
  THE BAR THAT JOIN RUNS ON -- on every edge. One weight for the whole popup left
  a 136 x 76 web hanging off a popup that had already gone to nothing on a side
  bar, and dropped it in one frame.
- **Children follow.** A popup drawn stretched toward a bar takes its child's
  ends with it, through a monotone map: an end inside by less than the stretch
  takes the whole move and lets go over twice it, an end hanging past has its
  hang scaled into the gap, and an end clamped at a bar stays. A drawn end never
  moves backwards while the real one moves forwards.
- **Voids.** A popup leaving a bar's span keeps flares at the facing ends that
  turn back into convex corners over `snapDistance`; the bar's lets go at most
  `1/settleMs` per ms, so it relaxes instead of snapping back. The room a flare
  leaves its corner is a limit the S at that corner is SOLVED against, never a
  cap applied afterwards (Corners of two bars, below: the crease).
- **Bars in a corner** (round 4, the user's request: "make this a feature where
  the system knows when to round out this inner corner"). Two perpendicular bars
  meeting at a screen corner round the corner between them with one fillet of
  `blendRadius`, whichever bar the corner square was given to: a bar whose whole
  END lies inside the other is drawn short of it (the side bar, so the square
  goes to the top/bottom bar), the pair touches, and the touch relation draws the
  corner. Before this only a side bar laid out to START under the top bar got it
  (test_bar's top-left) and one running through the square did not (its
  top-right, and bar_in_progress.qml's). Bars are static, so nothing about it
  animates. Bars that do not reach each other draw nothing (the bottom corners).
  A bar corner covered by another bar is square from the start and never treated
  as a corner still rounding in (that drew a popup growing out of the corner a
  hairline under the top bar). A flush end's outer corner is claimed sharp at
  once, which fixed a top bar with a side bar under EACH end leaving a scoop of
  air at the screen edge above one of them. A fillet bleeds back into its inner
  body no further than that body is solid behind the vertex (its extent less the
  end still rounding in and its corner at the other end on the line): with the
  side bar's end covered, a popup two pixels tall in the corner got a join at its
  top end whose 1.5 px bleed came out through its foot, a spur under the popup.
- **Facing fillets.** Two fillets on one face running toward each other -- the
  corner's fillet and the join of a popup a few pixels down the side bar,
  closing the channel under the top bar -- share the stretch between them in
  proportion to their reach, so the channel ends in one smooth C instead of the
  cusp both corners showed while such a popup opened; it flattens away as the
  channel closes. A face still parting from the line (a popup's top drawn a
  fraction of a pixel under the bar) counts as on it within the join's contact
  range, and the share fades with that offset: a 0.01 px cut-off flipped the
  channel's end between a C and two crossing arcs, 5 px apart, in one frame.
  The share comes in with the shallower fillet's depth (its side's length) over
  8 px: a popup with no height took its full share from the corner of two bars
  (Corners of two bars, below).
- **Seams between bars** reach 6 px into each bar (`BAR_SEAM`), not 1.5. The
  coverage is the distance over its screen-space gradient, which the GPU takes
  across a 2 x 2 pixel block, and a shallow seam over the flush ends at the
  screen's edge left the last column 5 % transparent for two rows at zoom 1
  (15 % at 0.5) -- on the right edge only, whose column is the second of its
  block.
- **Seams.** Where two faces touch along a line a thin rectangle covers it, placed
  on the resolved corners: only where both bodies are solid across its depth,
  stepping down to thinner seams toward an end that thins (a 1 px popup gets no
  1.5 px slab, and a lens-thin end no uncovered meeting row);
  as the faces part it withdraws from both ends over 4 px of gap, so the parting
  never shows as a full-length hairline and never shuts air in -- and where the
  channel is capped at one end by a third body (the slot under a top bar), it
  zips from that end instead. Touching void flares get one too. Fillets bleed
  across the line only while the faces touch.
- Surface tension is the old engine's (`dynamicMaxR` + `calcSquish`), per axis.
- The field is drawn only over the union of its primitives' boxes, cut into
  disjoint tiles that share exact edges, so nothing is drawn twice; at rest
  nothing runs (a settling join runs a FrameAnimation until it has let go).
- Pixels two device pixels inside the surface skip the rest of the evaluation.
  Nothing is a loop over a local array of uniforms: on iris that is a scratch
  copy of 240 floats per fragment, before any early-out
  (`tools/dev/perf/gooey_shader_gate.sh` fails on 240:10 spills:fills, 3 loops and
  248 memory sends; the generated nested guards in `tools/dev/gooey_field_ubo.py`
  keep it at 0:0 and 11).
- **The content is cut to the outline**: while a popup is opening, closing,
  moving, or has a face the field drew snapped, its content Loader is masked by
  `shaders/gooey_clip.frag` -- the same per-corner ellipse the surface is drawn
  with (`GooeyField.cornerRadii`, the DRAWN box), fed per slot by the engine.
  Otherwise content poked out of the corners surface tension had inflated. It is
  a `layer.effect`, so the content stays interactive; its texture is sized by
  `renderScale` (an FBO at item size is soft under a zoom); and it is DROPPED at
  rest, where the outline is the resting rounded rectangle that the design
  language's own radius rule already keeps clear. The drop is invisible for the
  GEOMETRY only: Qt Quick's default distance-field antialiasing is SUB-PIXEL,
  and a Text drawn into a layer comes out with other colour fringes than the
  same Text drawn directly (106-122 levels on the stroke edges, every font and
  size measured), so on the frame the layer drops every opaque Text in the
  popup changes its fringes -- the end of every open. With
  `QSG_DISTANCEFIELD_ANTIALIASING=gray` the two are the same picture. The host
  ACCEPTED that one-frame fringe change (round 2 phase 3): the shell keeps
  sub-pixel text, and `gooey_clip_switch.sh` judges the switch with gray
  text (CLAUDE.md: SUB-PIXEL TEXT IS NOT THE SAME PICTURE IN A LAYER). A popup
  whose outline at rest is not its box's rounded rectangle keeps the layer on
  purpose: the sized popup hangs 14 px past the bottom bar's end, where the
  join's S (an overhang shorter than `blendRadius` plus the popup's radius, 40)
  rounds its corner past `maxR` (32 px there). A face the field drew off its box
  at a bar's own end (notifs-right, 1 px short of its bar's top end) no longer
  happens at rest: such a box is placed on the end (The wall, below: THE BAR'S
  END).
- **And faded beside a fillet**: the outline cut leaves out what the field adds
  BEYOND a body, and wherever the field goes on past an edge a fold moves, over
  surface, the moving edge cut the content there straight across. Past the FAR
  edge that happens where a far corner joins something -- a perpendicular bar,
  a peer -- and its fillet runs on down it: the toast lane closing into its
  corner (and opening out of it) showed a card, its icon, a line of text ending
  on a line with surface below it (notifications round 2, laneCloseCut). Past a
  SIDE edge a 2D fold moves it always happens: at the face the S fillet into
  the host bar runs on beyond every side, and a popup the body sits beside melts
  into it -- the lane beside thePopup, closing sideways into its bar away from
  the popup, cut its header, body and bell straight down (verify f2). So while a
  body OPENS or CLOSES, its content fades out toward every edge the fold moves
  (the edges where the fold's two ends differ), the far edge only while a corner
  on it is drawn square (the field squares a corner only where it joins it):
  `edge_fade.frag` (the particles' mask; "nothing is cut hard") as a layer
  around the content's own (`GooeyBody.seamFade`, px per edge, set in
  `_applyClip`), over `blendRadius` -- the field's own reach past the box --
  times the fold's envelope along that edge's axis, min(1, 6e(1 - e)) of the
  fold's own fraction (SeamFade's), so it is nothing at either end: rest is
  untouched and the layer comes and goes at no fade. A far join whose fade has
  been drawn keeps it to the fold's end (`seamJoinRun` against `runSerial`, one
  per clock run): the field rounds that corner again where the fold is a few px
  thin, and the fade snapped off there over content still at 10-17 %. Alpha,
  not a band of the ground's colour: a tinted body's ground is poured across the
  very join that needs the fade. A resize (`moving`) takes none: its target
  changes under the envelope and the fade would switch on at full length. Gate:
  `gooey_lane_closecut.sh`.

What hosts use (backwards compatible — `bar_in_progress.qml` and the power
reel are unchanged): `thickness`, `maxR` (since 2026-09-24 only the defaults of
every bar's own thickness and radius), `surfaceColor` (now `Theme.surface`
by default), `renderScale`, `barsList`, `toggle(ownerId, barId, localCenter,
w, h, cornerAnim2D, component, widthFrom)`, `popupManager.open/close/toggle`,
`popupOpen`, `popupOwnerId`, `popupRadius` (the popup's free-corner radius,
`max(effRx, effRy)` as before), `thePopup.popX/popY/popW/popH`, `track.*`. Removed:
`seamBleed` (bar and popup are one field, there is no seam), the `*M_export`
metrics, the curtain Shape, the ghost `ShaderEffectSource`, `popupAbs*`.

New:

- **`w`/`h` may be `null`**: the popup follows its content's
  `implicitWidth`/`implicitHeight` on the 260 ms resize clock.
- **Opening out of the bar**: the closed box a popup opens from is clamped to
  its bar's span, so a part that will hang past the bar's end grows out of the
  bar (round 2, the content-sized popup).
- **One axis at a time**: in a corner migration the width changes only while
  travelling horizontally and the height only while travelling vertically.
- **The secondary popup**: `openChild(parentOwnerId, childOwnerId, side,
anchor, w, h, component, opts)`, `closeChild()`, `childOpen`,
  `childX/Y/W/H`. Everything about where it sits -- the anchor, its width, its
  inset -- eases on one slide clock from where it is drawn, so content that asks
  for a different size or place never teleports. Closing a popup whose secondary
  is out closes the SECONDARY first: the popup holds its box and its content
  until the secondary is `closeChildLead` (0.9) of the way in, and the secondary
  closes on the popup's own forward curve, so the two run into each other
  instead of the popup dragging an open secondary into the bar. `side` is `far` (away from the bar), `start`, `end` or an
  edge; `anchor` a point in the parent's content; `opts.overhang`,
  `opts.widthFrom`, `opts.key`, `opts.inset`. It follows the parent's box on
  every frame and closes with it. Wider than its parent it hangs past both sides
  (kept off the screen-edge bars); `inset` pulls its attach face that far into
  the parent -- the overlap belongs to the parent, drawn and clicked above it.
- **The content contract** (drop-in content opens a secondary without knowing
  the engine): `secondaryComponent`, `secondaryOpen`, `secondaryAnchor`,
  `secondarySide`, `secondaryOverhang`, `secondaryInset`, `secondaryKey`,
  `secondaryAnchorAt`, `secondaryWidthFrom` on the primary's root; `secondaryKey`
  on the secondary's root, assigned by the engine only while that content is
  faded out, and `secondaryCutout`, the rect of the secondary its primary covers,
  kept up to date for an inset secondary. A secondary may declare
  `function secondarySwapsInPlace(key)`: true for a key it swaps itself. The engine then
  skips its fade and assigns the key at once, in full view; the content takes what it
  wants faded out and back with `SwapCurtain` and keeps a chart out of it (stocks; weather's
  metrics that morph into each other, `WS.morphsInto`).
- **The anchor point and the corner open** (windowtray round 2, 2026-10-13):
  `secondaryAnchorAt` (0..1, default 0.5; `opts.anchorAt`) is WHICH point of the
  child sits on `secondaryAnchor` along the side -- 0 its start, 1 its end. That
  point holds still when the child's size changes, so a label that grows into a
  menu grows away from its top, position and size on the one slide clock with
  nothing translating (centred, the two moved at different paces: the user's
  "ugly disconnect"). The open grows out of that point: `secondaryWidthFrom` (the
  share of its length it opens at; default `childWidthFrom`) at 0 with an anchor
  at the start makes the child grow in 2D out of the corner its start makes with
  the parent's side -- a child laid against the bar face grows out of the inner
  corner of bar and parent, part of both from its first frame. At 0.5 / 0.5
  every box is what it was, to the byte.
- **The open point and the least open** (windowtray round 3, 2026-10-14):
  `secondaryOpenAt` (`opts.openAt`, default the anchor point) is the point the OPEN
  grows out of where that is not the point the child is held by -- a submenu held
  level with its row (anchor point 0) opens about its own centre (0.5), not from a
  point a pad above its row; `secondaryOpenMin` (`opts.openMin`, px, default 0) is
  the least length the open starts at, so a child no longer than that has its whole
  length from the first frame and only slides out of its parent (a one-row
  submenu). The open span stays clamped to the parent's side. Defaults: round 2's
  numbers exactly. Gate: `gooey_chain_probe.sh` (ARMs noopenat, noopenmin).
- **The pocket** (windowtray round 3; GooeyGeometry.js THE POCKET): a channel
  between a popup's face and a facing face (a bar's, its host's), capped at one end
  by a third body joined to both (a strip beside a rising label; the corner of two
  bars beside a popup sliding into it), zips shut from the cap as the drawn gap
  closes below `pocketReach` (2 x blendRadius): a fill runs toward the open end and
  the C that ended the channel at the cap rides its front, and over the last
  blendRadius of travel becomes the corner the join draws at contact (taken from the
  same pose solved with the guest flush), so no corner pops in at contact. Not between
  peers (lane, OSD), and not where the closing body's corners at the open end are
  pieces of another join's S -- unless that S is loose there (a straight run between
  its pieces) and its other body still: an island's END corner is the outer piece of
  the loose S its slim side bar makes at the island's other end, so a label reaching
  past the island's end keeps its pocket (refused, the pocket dropped and came back as
  the label's end crossed the island's: the hole the user saw flicker; whether it
  crossed depended on the focused window's title, which sets where the island ends).
  Gate: `tools/dev/tray_pocket_probe.sh` (the shell's own frame on mock services, the
  session's two tray items, titles that end the island before, inside and past the
  label's widening; every frame replayed through the geometry: the pocket on every
  channel frame, and the channel's cover never stepping faster than the motion moves
  it; ARM outcorner).
- **The child chain** (windowtray round 2): a child's content speaks the same
  contract to open the next level -- `childPopups` is [childSlot (the secondary),
  the tertiary, the quaternary], N levels as declared (one more is one more
  GooeyBody in the list). Each level is placed, drawn, joined and timed as the
  secondary is, against its PARENT's box; its owner id is the parent's +
  "/secondary"; a child's own `secondaryKey` is the engine's (what it shows), so it
  names its child's key `secondaryChildKey`. Closing a level closes every level
  below it first; a primary's close closes the chain. The field relates a level
  to its parent alone (GooeyGeometry HOST AND GUEST, by `parent` id: "child",
  "child2", "child3"), and the lane and the OSD keep out of thePopup and ALL its
  live descendants. Gate: `gooey_chain_probe.sh` (ARMs nochain, noanchor,
  nocorner, novoid, voidall).
- **A persistent secondary** (2026-09-24, for the stocks detail):
  `secondaryPersistent: true` on the primary's root. The content remembers
  that it was open (`modules/stocks/SecondaryMemory.qml`, per owner, for the
  shell's session, with a decay: 10 minutes for stocks) and comes up with
  `secondaryOpen` already true; the engine holds the child back while the popup
  grows and opens it `reopenChildLeadMs` before the popup lands -- the close
  played backwards (the close runs the child first and releases the popup when
  it is `closeChildLead` in; that last stretch is the lead: 42.8 ms with the
  default OutBack, 400 ms clocks). A popup closed before the release never opens
  it. Default off: without the flag a content that loads with `secondaryOpen`
  true opens its child at once, as before. Gates: `gooey_engine_probe_run.sh`
  (+`ARM=noreopenwait`), `stocks_popup_probe.sh` S19 (+ a second launch on the
  first one's state: the detail comes back after a boot; `ARM=nopersist`).
- **`secondaryMoving`** (2026-09-24): a secondary's root MAY declare it; the
  engine keeps it true while the child's box moves (opening, closing, sliding
  to a new anchor, easing a resize) and false at rest (each clock's last tick
  lays out once more, so it always ends false). Content holds expensive work
  until it rests: the stocks detail's chart, rebuilt when live data landed
  mid-slide, cost a missed frame on every swap (31-47 ms; ablated with
  `stocks_swap_probe.sh` -- the fetch, parse, disk and logo bake cost
  nothing visible, the rebuild all of it). Gate: `gooey_engine_probe_run.sh`.
- **The swipe-hold contract** (`swipeGestureActive`, `gestureHoldWidth/Height`)
  is honoured on the input mask.
- **The live contract** (2026-09-23, for the notes popup): see "Content contract:
  live resize, shift, room, tint, keyboard" below.
- **Input mask**: a body's exact box at rest; for a whole transition the union
  of where it came from and where it goes at the easing's extremes, so the
  compositor hears about it twice per animation, not every frame. The rectangle
  a closed popup used to leave behind is gone.
- Every tunable is on `test_bar.qml`'s Engine tab.

Drop-in contract for module content (what every `test_<module>.qml` is built against): a `Component` whose root `Item` works with `anchors.fill: parent`, owns its own state, and advertises `implicitWidth` / `implicitHeight`. The Loader is emptied once a popup has closed, so module state that must survive a close (an update run, the clock's last tab, the long-lived TerminalBackend) lives outside the content, in the module's bar-side object. Existing drop-ins: `modules/clock/ClockPopupContent.qml`, `modules/battery/BatteryPopup.qml`, `modules/bar/system_update/components/SystemUpdatePopup.qml`, `AudioPlayerPopup.qml`, `LlmPopup.qml`. Bar-side pieces: `modules/clock/ClockBarButton.qml`, `modules/bar/battery/BatteryBarIcon.qml`, `modules/bar/audio/AudioBarButton.qml`, `modules/bar/system_update/components/SystemUpdateIcon.qml`, `modules/bar/workspaces/Workspaces.qml`, `modules/bar/reel/ReelBarModule.qml`.

## Content contract: live resize, shift, room, tint, keyboard

Added 2026-09-23 for the notes popup, which the user resizes by dragging its free

**How a drag should use it.** At press, read `hostRoom` (`a0`, `a1`, `lo`, `hi`,
`out`, `face`) and the box's out-extent, and set `liveResizeActive`. On a move,
compute the grabbed edge from the pointer and keep the other at its press value:
along the bar, `newAlong = |grabbed - fixed|` clamped so both ends stay inside
`lo..hi`, and `alongShift = (fixed + grabbed) / 2 - center` -- the centre is
`center + shift` exactly as long as the box is inside `lo..hi`, so the clamp never
engages and the fixed edge does not creep; out of the bar, the size is simply the
pointer's distance from `face`, clamped to `out`. Write width, height and shift
in any order: whatever a frame collects is laid out ONCE, in that frame's
polish, and drawn in that frame (below). `hostRoom.a0/a1` of the new box are there
on the next event, not in the handler that wrote the size. Release: clear
`liveResizeActive` (after the last size, or in the same handler -- both work).
A content that animates its OWN box to a new size (a morph) asks
`hostRoom.restAt(along, shift)` where that size will rest and lands there: the
host then has nothing left to move. And a drag's owner that wants the dragged
edge on a wall (not the whole box slid onto it) reads `wallLo` / `wallHi` /
`snap` (`EdgeResize.wallSnap`).

**Once per frame, in the frame.** A follow is not laid out in the change handler
(a drag's three writes were three layouts) nor with `Qt.callLater` (that runs
after EACH pointer event: measured 1.56 layouts per frame at three events per
frame; a 1000 Hz mouse on the 120 Hz panel would be up to eight). `_requestFollow` pokes a hidden `Row`
(`followHook`); a positioner lays itself out in the polish phase, which runs
before the frame is synchronised, and its `positioningComplete` runs the follow
there. Under the threaded render loop (what quickshell runs, offscreen included)
changes from pointer events, timers and animation ticks all land in the next
frame's polish, so the box, the surface and the content's own layout are drawn
together. Measured (`gooey_liveresize_probe.sh`): at most one layout per
frame on all four edges, the engine's side of a live frame 0.70 ms (0.55 of it
`fieldShape`, the geometry every animated frame pays; `GooeyField` now writes
only the uniforms that changed and none past the counts, which took a layout from
0.94 to 0.66 ms).

**The tint.** `GooeyField.tints: [{ body, color }]` (up to three bodies) and
`tintBlend` (px, default 20, `engine.tintBlend`). The shader keeps the shape
exactly as it was -- coverage decides every edge -- and only the colour of a
covered pixel changes: each body's BOX distance, taken outside the box only,
goes into the group of the tint it carries or the untinted rest, and the groups
are weighted `(1 - x / tintBlend)^2` by how much farther than the nearest they
are. Inside a popup the bar's share then depends on the distance from the bar
alone: one smooth band `tintBlend` deep on each side of the face, a 45-degree
line through the fillet, a circle round the corners, the popup exactly its tint
beyond it (up to its free outline) and the bar exactly the surface. Which bodies
carry a tint sits in the uniform block's padding after `showTiles` (no member
moves); the colours reach the fragment shader as varyings from the vertex
shader, so no new uniform is pulled: `tools/dev/perf/gooey_shader_gate.sh` stays at
0:0 spills and 11 / 11 / 12 sends (SIMD8 / 16 / 32). An untinted field takes one
branch not taken. A first version passed the three body ids as varyings too and
SIMD32 stopped register-allocating; a second weighted by the signed distance and
the ramp ran twice as deep down the popup's free sides.

## Every bar its own thickness (2026-09-24, the launcher's slim bar)

The launcher grows out of a 6 px full-width bottom bar under a 40 px top bar, and
the user's rule for it: any thickness (2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 20, 40, 48 ...) must work by
changing ONE property. So a bar carries its own numbers and everything placed
against a bar reads THAT bar's; the engine's `thickness` and `maxR` survive only
as the defaults, and with every bar at them the engine draws the same bytes as
before (`gooey_thickness_bytes.sh BEFORE=<rev>`).

`GooeyBar { thickness; radius; reserve }`:

| member      | default            | what follows it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `thickness` | `engine.thickness` | the bar's box; its popups' face (`_boxAt(info)`, hostRoom `face`); how far a popup on a PERPENDICULAR edge stays off it at that edge's end (`_endInsets`: that bar's own thickness, 0 where there is none); the 2D open's corner point; the wall a migration rides past it; a secondary's limits across the edge it is on; the content clip's growth past its face; the reel's bar cell |
| `radius`    | `engine.maxR`      | the bar's free corners -- only those: a corner with a side on the SCREEN'S EDGE is square (below), and a free corner beside a square one rounds across the bar's whole thickness (a 6 px bar of radius 20 ending mid-screen ends in one 20 x 6 curve from its face to the screen's edge)                                                                                                |
| `reserve`   | `thickness`        | `engine.edgeReserve[edge]` = the largest reserve on the edge (0 for a bare edge), which `HyprReserve` (the shell) or `GooeyEdgeReservers` (a bare host) hands the compositor. The slim bar sits inside Hyprland's `gaps_out` with `reserve: 0`: windows keep their place                                                                                                                                                           |

The sites, in `GooeyEngine.qml`:

- `track.getBarInfoById` returns `thickness` and `bar` too. `track.barPast(corner,
dir, reach)` returns the perpendicular bar found `reach` px past a corner, where
  reach = `track.edgeThickness(host edge) + 2`: the corner square is as deep as the
  bars on the edge the question is asked from (42 px past a 40 px top bar's corner,
  8 past a 6 px bottom bar's). `edgeThickness(edge)` is the thickest bar on an edge.
- `_limits` = the edge less `_endInsets(edge)`; `_closestCorner` steps into the
  inner corner by the same insets.
- `hostRoom.out = extent - host thickness - edgeThickness(opposite) - 12`.
- Migrations: `_cornerPos(cn)` puts the box against `_cornerWall(cn)`, per axis
  `_edgeWall(edge, cn)` = the thickness of that edge's bar NEAREST the corner (0 on
  a bare edge). `P.move` records `sf` / `ef`, how far the start box's face is off
  its edge and the end box's off its own, and `_polyAt` pins each leg's face to its
  wall EASED between the leg's two ends (`sf` -> first corner, corner -> corner,
  last corner -> `ef`). So a 40 px top bar round a side bar to a 6 px bottom bar
  rides every wall, and a popup passing two bars of different thickness on one
  edge moves from the one to the other instead of jumping.
- `_childTarget` keeps a wide secondary between `edgeThickness` of the two edges
  across its axis.
- A bar's rect or radius changing (`_barRects`, which a thickness change moves)
  re-places every live popup at once (`_relayoutTargets`): retuning a bar under
  an open popup moves the popup on the next frame, without an ease.
  `onThicknessChanged` went: a default change reaches the engine through the bars.

The surface already worked per body and needed nothing: a bar's corner is clamped
to half its own extent per axis; the fillet of a popup hanging from a bar sits in
the AIR on the popup's side, so the bar's thickness never enters it (a circle of
`blendRadius` at every T, measured); the corner fillet of a side bar and a slim

## Square on the screen's edge (2026-09-24, the user)

"The bars should not be rounded on the sides that would be flush with the
screen's edges." Every bar: a corner with either of its sides on the screen's
edge is SQUARE -- the side a bar stands on, and an end that reaches the screen's
perpendicular edge. A full-width bar is square all round; a bar ending
mid-screen rounds only the corners of that end away from the screen, and there
the curve may run across the WHOLE thickness ("the curve beginning at the top
edge of the tiny bar, ending at the bottom edge aka the screen's edge"), since
the corner below it is square. `GooeyEngine._barSquare` works the list out from
each bar's rect against the screen and hands it to the geometry as the body's
`square` ([tl, tr, br, bl]), which starts those corners `covered` (the square
the BARS IN A CORNER rule already drew) and gives each free corner its own
radii (`fRx`/`fRy`). Popups and the joins are unchanged. Gate:
`gooey_bar_corners_render.sh` (square screen corners, the slim bar's
ends rounding at the face and across its whole thickness, a side bar's free
end; the engine before this fails it).

A square corner then showed the field's own flaw (2026-09-25, found by the
notifications session as "a 3 px notch at the top-right screen corner"): the
coverage is the distance over its screen-space gradient, and on the 2x2 quad at
a square corner the derivatives straddle the box's inner diagonal and read
sqrt(2), so the corner's three pixels came out at 0.85 wherever the quad fell
that way -- the screen's top-right corner of a full-width bar, never its
top-left. Rounded, no bar corner had sat there. `gooey_field.frag` caps the
gradient at the linear pixel size (a unit-gradient distance never has more);
the same cap takes the 0.15 of ink a crease (a fillet's tangent with the bar's
face) put outside the outline. Against a 4x supersampled render the changed
pixels' error fell from 0.035 to 0.012 (max 0.145 to 0.032). Gate: the corner
render's S4 reads every device pixel of each square screen corner; the
harness had been blind to it, see CLAUDE.md on grabs over nothing.

## The smooth group and the bar ripple (2026-09-26, launcher round 3)

Two field primitives for the launcher's confirm flourish (plan Stage 2, the
REVIEW OUTCOME: the fillet-neck bridge E4 replaced by the smooth group, the
ripple E5 amended). Both live in `GooeyField` and the field shader only --
fieldShape relates nothing new -- and both are OFF by default, and when off the
field draws the bytes it drew before them (gated against 9854fd5).

**The smooth group** (`GooeyField.smoothMask`, bit i = `bodies[i]`;
`smoothK`, px). The flagged bodies leave the hard union and blend with EACH
OTHER by the classic quadratic smooth-min, `h = max(k - |a - b|, 0) / k`,
`smin = min(a, b) - h^2 k / 4`, chained in slot order; the group joins the rest
by the hard min. It is the metaball neck: two members a gap g apart are joined
while g < k/2, the neck narrows to a point at exactly k/2, and nothing is left
between them from k/2 + 0.5 on. smin's gradient is a convex mix of the two it
blends, so the capped-gradient coverage holds; a member is skipped only when its
box lies a whole k behind the group (smin returns the group exactly there).
Fewer than two members among the bodies, or `smoothK <= 0`: nothing changes.
GooeyField adds tiles round each member (its box grown by k/4 + 4.5: smin never
lies more than k/4 below the min), less what the shape's own tiles cover -- the

along each 2x2 quad's lower image row, dFdy down its left column -- the per-row

10.5 for two 200 px boxes, 100.7 / 52.8 / 0 for a 120 px row over a wide box. The

**The ripple** (`GooeyField.rippleBody` (-1 off), `rippleFace` (0 top,
1 bottom, 2 left, 3 right: the face that moves; the launcher's bottom bar moves
its top), `rippleTime` (s), `rippleSource0..2` = (s along the face, start
offset t0, amplitude A, -), `rippleParams` (the globals, `GooeyRipple.DEFAULTS`),
`ripple` (what it drew: params, frame, band)). The flagged bar's own distance
carries the displaced face, because troughs remove material and the union
cannot:

- h = win(t) [ B e^{-(s - sb)^2 / 2w^2} e^{-t/tauB} + sum_i sum_images
  A_i e^{-gamma tt} e^{-((d - c tt)/sigma)^2} cos(k (d - c tt) + k c (1 - phaseRatio) tt) ],
  tt = t - t0_i, d = sqrt((s - q)^2 + a^2) (a smooth source, no V at the
  impact). The envelope rides at the group speed c, the carrier at phaseRatio c
  (2/3, capillary), so crests run through the packet instead of riding it.
- Reflections: the images of each impact point in the bar's two ends (Neumann),
  unrolled by reflection order up to 4 and nested by `rippleOrders`, which
  `GooeyRipple.imageCount` derives from L, c, sigma and Tend.
- win(t) = 1 - smoothstep(Tend - fade, Tend, t): h is EXACTLY 0 from Tend on,
  and there GooeyField switches the ripple off -- the idle bytes again.
- A round end keeps the wave off its corner (the corner's ellipse would cut a
  crest with a step) and brings it back over as far again; a square end keeps
  it to the wall. Troughs are floored by tanh at the bar's thickness - 1: a
  pixel of bar always remains.
- The moving side's distance is taken along the face's normal and divided by
  sqrt(1 + h'^2), h' analytic: a unit gradient however steep the flank.
- The rippling body always sits in shader slot 0 (GooeyField permutes the slots
  while it ripples and remaps the tints and the group's bits) and is evaluated
  LAST, with its early-out box grown by `GooeyRipple.crestBound` (a hard bound,
  every term at most its amplitude, plus a pixel). GooeyField appends the band
  over the crests' reach (the bar's span, from the crest bound past the face to
  its far side) less what the shape's tiles cover; fieldShape's tiles are left
  as they are.
- `GooeyRipple.js` is the JS twin, term for term: `height(s, t, p)`,
  `slope(s, t, p)`, `crestBound(p)`, `imageCount(p)`, and `frame(p, t)`, the
  per-frame reduction GooeyField writes (damping, onset, window, phase: time
  alone). `params(globals, geometry(body, face), sources)` builds p the way the
  field does; the host keeps joins on the moving face with it.

Measured (`gooey_ripple_render.sh`, 37 states on a 6 px full-width bar, a

**Easings (E1).** `Geo.EASING_NAMES` gains 6 InQuad, 7 InCubic, 8 InBack
(appended: an index is a saved value). InBack is Qt's, `overshoot` in Qt's
units; `_easeRange` takes its dip below 0 into the input mask like OutBack's

**The radius override (E2).** A body may carry `radii: [tl, tr, br, bl]`, each
a number or `[rx, ry]`, instead of `rx`/`ry` (`GooeyGeometry.fieldShape`): a
round body larger than 40 px (tensionRadii's cap is not applied to it) and a
rounded FOOT only. A corner of 0 counts as square for the square-neighbour rule,
so `[0, 0, r, r]` on a body w x h is a foot of two quarter ellipses w/2 x min(r,
h), and `[20, 20, 60, 60]` on 120 x 100 a 60 x 50 half-ellipse under a rounded
top. The field clamps each corner to its half of the side (the whole side beside
a square one), as for every body; `freeAlong` answers an overridden body per
corner. The engine gives it to the flourish (opts.radii, setFlourish) and to the
droplets (r all round); no slot uses it.

**Free bodies.** `free: true` on a body relates it to NOTHING in fieldShape: no
snapping, no joins, no fillets, no seams, no flares, no end corner, and it caps
no channel -- it is drawn at its box with its own corners, and its only
relation is the field's smooth group. The flourish and its droplets are free.
Snapped, a bead 10 px over a bar's face was drawn 26 px tall, a thread (the
review's F5); the probe's ARM `snapping` pulls the ball's foot 17.7 px onto the
face and stretches every droplet.

**A close of its own.** `close(opts)`, opts `{ ms, easingIndex, overshoot, hold }`
for that close (defaults openMs, openEasingIndex, openOvershoot: `close()` is
the close it always was). `hold: true` holds it -- the box stays where it is (at
rest or not), the content fades now, `closeHeld` and `GooeyBody.flourishHold`
are true -- until `releaseClose()`, when it runs on the clock it asked for
(`_waitsForFlourish` in `_releaseCloses`, beside `_waitsForChild`; the fade that
already runs is left to finish). The flag is set before `hold()`: hold() moves t,
t lays out, and the layout's own `_releaseCloses` let an unflagged hold go in the
same call (the first version's bug; the probe's close check caught it). A reopen
by the same owner while held resumes the popup and fades its content back in;
another module's open lets it go, as a held-for-secondary close is let go. Why
(the review's F1): under OutBack 400 ms the launcher's top face leaves at ~5600
px/s and any neck lasts 1-2 frames (ARM `nooverride`: 4 ms).

**The group eases** (2026-09-27). `setFlourishGroup` used to take effect in one
frame: `['host','flourish']` at k 40 with the flourish 12 px inside the host's
sides bulged the host out by 4.9 px a side in one frame (a smooth-min's dent is
(k - d)^2 / 4k at a distance d < k), and a host leaving the group (a reopen)
took its neck off the same way. Now the group the field draws is eased to the
one asked for on the effects' clock, so no call pops (`_easeGroup`, every
layout; the step in `_effectsAt`):

- its k moves toward the k asked for at no more than `groupRate` (333 px/s: a k
  of 40 comes and goes in 120 ms). A dent grows by at most a quarter of its k's
  change, so no outline moves more than 1.4 px a side in a 60 Hz frame. A rate
  and not a time constant: an owner's own smooth ramp slower than the rate is
  followed exactly (the bridge's is, to the pixel: the neck measures as before),
  and no call, however large, moves more than the rate a frame;
- a body already drawn that ENTERS or LEAVES the group does so at k 0: the k
  goes down with the old members, the members change there (a group at k 0 draws
  the bytes of none), and it comes back up with the new ones. A swap of members
  at k 40 takes 240 ms; a member change beside bodies too far apart to blend
  shows nothing for the first half;
- a body NEW this layout (the flourish opening, a droplet thrown) joins at once,
  because where a new body first appears is its owner's choice, as for any body
  (the droplets are thrown from inside the merge); a body that is GONE leaves at
  once;
- fewer than two members drawn is no group and k 0, so a group always grows
  from nothing -- the host joining when its close starts included.

`'host'` becomes a present body entering when the close starts, so an owner may
name the group before the close and at its full k: the host still comes in from
k 0.

**The droplets' budget** (2026-09-27). Droplets were pushed after every slot, so
a popup opening on a full field took the last droplet's body index and the
droplet vanished in one frame. It cannot shrink out FIRST: the popup's body is
drawn from its first frame (at 50 ms an OutBack open is 44 % of its size), so
the index it takes has to be free already. Droplets therefore leave
`dropletReserve` (1) index free when they are thrown; a body that needs one while
they fly takes the reserve at once, and the last droplet still whole then SHRINKS
OUT over `dropletShrinkMs` (60 ms, 3-4 frames: its radius to 0 on the effects'
clock) and frees its index, which is the reserve back. A droplet that leaves
before it lands takes its landing's ripple source with it (its amplitude 0), so
no wave comes out of a bar where nothing fell. Only a second body inside those
60 ms finds no index, and then the last droplet still goes at once.

and the ripple in one batch, and the field is laid out once when it returns
instead of once per property.

### The neck (launcher round 4)

`setFlourishNeck(null | {a, mu, l, ml, rA, qU, rL, qL, kA, kB, kMid, kJoin})`: an
explicit bridge between the flourish (side A) and the closing `host` (side B), drawn
INSTEAD of the smooth group between them. Why: the group blends the card with the launcher
directly, so the card's corners bulge towards it however thin the neck, the waist sits by
the flat face and its corners are whatever the saddle's asymptotes make them.

`N = smin(armU, armL, kMid)`; `d = smin(smin(dA, N, kA), smin(dB, N, kB), kJoin)`. An
arm is iq's exact uneven capsule from a wide end inside its body to a tip at the waist,
CUT beyond the wide circle's far point (`max(cap, r - t)`): a shape inside a body is not
nothing to a smooth-min (it lowers the distance wherever the two are within k, raising a
bump of (1 - m/k)^2 k/4 on the body's other edges, 5 px measured), so the arm meets its
body in a flat face a blend radius deep. The card and the launcher are never blended with
each other; `kJoin` only rounds where the two sides meet.

skipped) and the post-loop term replaces `d = min(d, ds)`. The 17 numbers are `#define`d
(generated, `gooey_field_ubo.py` NECK) onto the RIPPLE's pushed lanes, so no uniform is
added (gate: 0 spills, 11 sends, SIMD16): the two are exclusive -- GooeyField draws no
ripple while a neck is, the engine fades a running one (`_rippleCut`), and `isB` / the
post term are guarded by `rippleMode`, which a ripple's `crest` lane (= `neckB`) would
otherwise fool. Neck off: every lane 0, the same bytes (smooth and ripple renders,

## The OSD slot (2026-10-03)

The OSD is a transient popup of its own that comes out of a bar when something
changes (volume, brightness, a track, ...), dips into the bar and comes back with
other content when the kind changes while it is out, and lives shorter than
anything it meets -- the user's rule: it is the one that moves. A sixth `GooeyBody`
(`osdSlot`, `engine.theOsd`; `isOsd`, `dipping`, `yieldSides`, `peerIds`), built
like the lane and never the primary, the spare or a child. Contract:
`docs/contracts/osd.md` section 5.

**The dip.** `_dipOsd`: the box FOLDS into its bar from where it is drawn now --
`_laneFace` of the current box, narrowed about its centre by `osdDipWidthTo`, phase
`closing` with `dipping` set, so the clip, the seam fade and the sticking joins are
the close's own and the content fades out (`min(contentFadeMs, osdDipFoldMs)`). It is
NOT `_openFresh`, which jumps the box to the origin: the rise must start where the
fold ended (the no-jump rule; `jumprise` is the ARM). At the bottom (`_slotFinished`,
`_osdBottom`) the engine emits `osdSwap` FIRST, then sets the component and key (the
Loader is synchronous, so the implicit size is known), places, and rises from the
folded face over `osdDipRiseMs`. The fold's last tick and the swap are ONE frame, so a
frame sampled in `dipping` never shows the folded box: the owner is told at it, and a
probe reads the box inside the signal. Interrupts: another kind during the fold only
updates the request (the bottom uses the latest, ONE dip); the shown component and key
asked for again during the fold cancels it (`_osdUndip`: rise from where the fold is,
no `osdSwap`); another kind during the rise dips again from where the box is. A hop,
a place across a popup that is out, or no room at the bottom finishes the fold and
opens fresh (or waits) where it goes now. `closeOsd` during a fold lets the fold go
on as a plain close.

**Where it goes: it yields.** `_osdEvent` runs after EVERY `_laneEvent` (the lane's
placement is now `_laneEventBody` behind a wrapper that places the lane, then the OSD),
in `closeLane`, in `_slotFinished` of anything that was in its way, and in
`_relayoutTargets` (`_osdRelayout`). The priority is the user's:

1. **Glide**: `Geo.yieldPlace(spot, size, obstacles, limits)` (pure, node-tested in
   `gooey_yield_cases.js`, 4000 fuzz cases against a 0.25 px scan) finds the free
   place nearest home -- home, each obstacle cluster's two sides (obstacles that leave
   less room than it needs between them are one cluster), the two limits -- with a tie to
   the side it is on. Obstacles: thePopup's TARGET, a popup holding its close, the child's
   target, the lane's target (`_osdObstacles`). A box on the OSD's own bar keeps no gap
   (it melts); a perpendicular one keeps `blendRadius` (no peer). It glides on `osdGlideMs`
   (the resize clock when thePopup's own resize caused it, so the faces move as one).
2. **Displace the lane** (`osdDisplaceLane`): nothing fits with the lane in the way but
   home is free without it AND the lane still finds a place around it
   (`_laneFitsAround`: its own `Geo.lanePlace`, and it must get there without passing
   thePopup): `_osdOverLane`, which `_laneObstacles` reads, and the lane is
   placed again (`_laneEventBody`) -- it glides around the OSD, or folds as `laneBlocked`
   (its owner freezes the toast clocks already). Cleared, and the lane placed again, when
   the OSD closes or is blocked.
3. **Push thePopup** (`osdPushPopup`): the arrangement that moves thePopup least
   (`_osdPushPlan`): the OSD's nearest free place ignoring thePopup and its child -- and,
   when they share an axis, the places flush against thePopup as it would stand pushed to
   either end of its limits and unpushed, and the OSD's own limits (the nearest free place alone
   is home, in the MIDDLE of a bar, where thePopup has nowhere to go: the mockup's 300 px popup
   at 0.5 of a 720 px bar with a 300 px OSD was `osdBlocked` for it) -- each tried with the
   smallest shift `Geo.pushPlace` gives (thePopup's own bar, inside its limits, AWAY from the
   OSD, never through it; an OSD already out keeps its side). The least shift wins, a tie the
   place nearer home. It is `GooeyBody.osdYield` (screen px along the bar), added to the rest
   place in `_targetsOf` and in `_restAt` -- so `hostRoom.restAt` still answers truthfully --
   and eased on the resize clock; the lane then places itself around the OSD's target too
   (`_osdPins`: it is part of the arrangement), and folds `laneBlocked` if the bar has no room
   left for all three. Folding the lane is for when NOTHING fits (the user: "one or the other,
   or both reposition according to the situation and available space so that they border each
   other and melt"): the plan ranks arrangements first by whether the lane keeps a place and
   gets there without crossing thePopup (`laneFits`), then by the smallest push, and the
   candidates include the OSD flush against the lane at either end of the bar; a lane that stays
   is an obstacle thePopup may not be pushed onto. So the lane at 0.12 (140), thePopup 300 and
   the OSD 300 on a 754 px bar (740) stay a row of three -- the lane glides 14 px, thePopup 73,
   the OSD takes the end -- and a 420 px popup (840) still folds the lane (the fallback is the
   arrangement that moves least). The lane is named a peer of an OSD it was displaced for even
   though it arrives after the OSD was placed (a peer's weight is the gap's, so naming it early
   costs nothing): left out, the two antialiased fills met on a line and drew a grey row across
   the join. Never a popup that `continuesBarCell`, one being resized by hand
   (`_liveOf`), one travelling or closing. The popup's own boxes are read WITHOUT the push
   (`_unpushed`), so the same facts give the same answer at every event and a pushed popup is
   not an obstacle by being pushed. Back to 0 when the OSD closes (`_osdRelease`) or a journey
   starts.
4. **Wait**: `osdBlocked`; it folds if it was out, keeps its request and content, and opens
   the moment a popup that was in its way comes to rest or leaves (`_slotFinished`).

Like the lane it never passes THROUGH a popup: a new place on its far side is a dip and a
fresh open there; and it waits while a popup that is out still has to pass where it would be.

**No overlap on any frame, and who gives way.** In `layout()` the OSD's box (and the box its
